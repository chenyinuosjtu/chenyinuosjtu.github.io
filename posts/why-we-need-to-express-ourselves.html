Why we need to express ourselves

August 2026

We need to express ourselves. Otherwise, much of who we are remains invisible. Our concerns, our contributions, and the thoughts that matter to us stay inside our own heads, where no one else has the chance to meet them.

Sometimes that invisibility can make me question my own existence—not literally, but socially. If I never show what I am thinking or working through, how can anyone understand where I am, what I need, or what I might offer?

Expression is especially important when we need help. People cannot respond to a problem they cannot see. When I can name my difficulty clearly, share the context, and explain what I have tried, I make it possible for someone else to understand me and perhaps help me move forward.

It also creates a way to stay in sync with other people. The more often I make my thinking visible, the more chances there are for others to recognise something familiar, correct my assumptions, contribute their perspective, or simply say, “I have been there too.” Over time, those small exchanges make connection and help more likely.

To express myself is not to demand attention. It is to make understanding possible.
