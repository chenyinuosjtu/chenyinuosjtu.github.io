Why we need to keep creating

August 2026

We should keep creating things. A piece of writing, a sketch, a prototype, a small tool, or a set of notes can turn something invisible into something we can return to.

An artifact gives form to our thoughts, skills, and experience. While they remain only in our minds, they are difficult to examine and easy to lose. By making something, we create an abstraction: a smaller, clearer version of what we have learned or discovered.

That matters when we come back after time has passed. Instead of starting from nothing, we can use the artifact as a way back in. It preserves context, decisions, and questions. It lets us restart quickly, continue the work, or see it with fresh eyes.

Artifacts are also checkpoints. They make the progress of our work visible—not as a final verdict on our ability, but as evidence of where we have been and what we have tried. They give other people something concrete to understand, discuss, improve, and build on.

Creating does not always mean making something large or complete. Often, the small things are enough: a note that captures an idea, a draft that clarifies a question, or a prototype that makes a possibility real. Each one leaves a trace that makes the next step easier.
